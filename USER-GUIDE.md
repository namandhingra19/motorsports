# Research Workspace — how to use it

A short guide to the app we use for our weekly research. Read it once, keep it for reference.

**Address:** `http://<your-team-address>` — whoever set it up will send you the link.
**Your account:** your name and a starting password from the admin. Change it as soon as you sign in
(sidebar, bottom left, the pencil next to your name).

---

## The idea in one minute

We do not each research a different topic. Every week the team picks **one** topic. That topic is
split into **branches** (areas), each branch gets **tasks**, and everyone fills their branches with
**findings**, **sources**, **notes** and **questions** as they work.

If you stumble on something more interesting than the topic itself, you do not throw away the
research or derail anyone — you create a **new branch**, and it becomes researchable on its own.

At the weekend we meet, sort what we found, and write down what we decided. Then the week is
archived and a new topic starts.

```
Topic  ->  branches  ->  tasks  ->  findings / sources / notes
                     \
                      -> new ideas -> opportunities -> decisions
```

## Getting around

The dark column on the left is the menu. Everything you need is there, and every screen shows the
week currently selected in the dropdown at the top right.

| Screen | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | The week at a glance: topic, progress, your tasks, latest findings, new ideas, open questions. Start here. |
| **Research tree** | The full structure of the topic. Click any branch to open it. |
| **My work** | Only your tasks, split into overdue, due today, upcoming and done — plus everything you have contributed. |
| **All tasks** | Every task in the week, with filters by person, status, priority, branch and deadline. |
| **Ideas** | The branches that were discovered mid-research rather than planned. |
| **Findings** | Everything the team now believes, filterable by confidence and tag. |
| **Resources** | Every source anyone has attached, searchable and filterable by type. |
| **Opportunities** | Branches written up as possible businesses, arranged by how far along they are. |
| **Decisions** | What we decided and why, numbered, so we do not re-argue settled questions. |
| **Meeting** | Seven questions to answer before and during the weekly discussion. |
| **Weekly summary** | The one page that captures the whole week. The admin writes it; everyone reads it. |
| **Team** | Who is in the workspace and how loaded everyone is this week. |
| **Archive** | Past weeks. Open one to read its full tree and conclusions. |

**Search** (top bar) covers branches, tasks, findings, sources, notes, questions, opportunities and
people in the selected week. Results tell you where each hit lives, and clicking one takes you there.

## Your week, step by step

### Monday — pick up your work
1. Open **Dashboard** and read the topic and the objective. That objective is what we are trying to
   answer by Sunday.
2. Look at **My work**. Your tasks are there, with deadlines.
3. If something is unclear, open the branch and leave a comment in **Discussion** rather than
   guessing.

### Tuesday to Thursday — research and write as you go
Open the branch you are working in. Along the top you will find eight tabs:

- **Overview** — what the branch covers, its progress, who is on it.
- **Tasks** — what needs doing here. Change the status yourself: `TODO → IN PROGRESS → REVIEW →
  COMPLETED`, or `BLOCKED` if you are stuck. Use `BLOCKED` early; it is how the admin knows to help.
- **Findings** — the important one. A finding is *one thing you now believe, and why*. Give it a
  title someone can scan, a couple of sentences of substance, a confidence level (`LOW`, `MEDIUM`,
  `HIGH`) and any sources that back it up.
- **Resources** — every link you used, with a one-line note on why it matters and a type
  (`PAPER`, `REPORT`, `COMPANY`, `PATENT`, `PRODUCT`, `NEWS`, …). Add the source *before* the finding
  and you can tick it as supporting evidence.
- **Notes** — observations, methods, warnings, meeting notes. Anything that is useful but is not a
  finding.
- **Questions** — what we do not know yet. Anyone can answer later and mark it answered.
- **Discussion** — comments on the branch itself.
- **Opportunities** — the business case, if this branch has grown into one.

**The rule that makes the whole thing work:** write the finding when you find it, not on Sunday
night. Research nobody wrote down did not happen.

### Found something better? Branch it
Click **+ New branch / idea** on the dashboard, or **Sub-branch** inside a branch. Give it a title,
say *why it is interesting*, and choose where it sits in the tree. It becomes a normal branch with
its own tasks, findings and sources — and it shows up on the **Ideas** screen with your name on it.
Nothing in the original research moves or breaks.

### Friday — tidy up
- Finish or honestly mark your tasks.
- Reread your branch: is what you learned actually written down as findings?
- Put anything you could not resolve into **Questions**.
- Add your points to **Meeting** under the seven prompts. Two minutes of thought here saves twenty
  minutes of meeting.

### Weekend — the discussion
We go through **Meeting** together. The admin marks each point `KEEP`, `INVESTIGATE`, `REJECT` or
`DECISION NEEDED`. Anything that needs a call gets written into **Decisions** with the reason.
Promising branches get converted into **Opportunities**, and the admin writes the **Weekly summary**
and closes the week.

## Findings, opportunities, decisions — what goes where

- **Finding:** something you learned. *"High-grade magnets are almost entirely imported."*
- **Opportunity:** a branch that might be a business. Click **Convert to opportunity** on any branch
  and answer as much as you can: problem, customer, existing solution, market, pain level,
  willingness to pay, technology, competition, why now, why us, moat, MVP cost, regulation,
  scalability, open questions. **Blanks are honest** — an empty field is a research task, not a
  failure. Opportunities move through `EXPLORING → VALIDATING → PROMISING → SHORTLISTED`, or
  `REJECTED` when we have decided against them.
- **Decision:** a settled question. *"Should we investigate robotic actuators further? Yes, because…"*

## What each role can do

**Everyone can:** see everything, update their own tasks, add findings, sources, notes, questions and
comments, create new branches and ideas, convert a branch into an opportunity, and search the whole
week.

**Admins can also:** create and edit the week, create and assign tasks, rename, move or delete
branches, log decisions, mark verdicts in the meeting, write the weekly summary, close and reopen
weeks, and manage members.

When a week is closed it turns read-only for everyone — the grey bar at the top tells you so. The
work stays fully readable and searchable in **Archive** forever. If it was closed too early, an admin
can reopen it from the Archive screen.

## Small things worth knowing

- The workspace is shared and live. Other people's changes appear on their own within about fifteen
  seconds; reload if you want them immediately.
- Nothing is private. Every finding, note and comment carries your name — that is the point.
- Deleting is limited on purpose: you can remove your own findings, sources and notes; only an admin
  can delete tasks, branches and decisions.
- A branch with no findings after a week is a signal, not a failure. Say so at the meeting.
- Use tags consistently (`pricing`, `imports`, `manufacturing`). They are how the Findings screen
  becomes useful in week six.

## Quick answers

**I cannot sign in.** Names are exact but not case-sensitive. Ask the admin to reset your password
from the Team screen.

**I cannot edit anything.** Check the week dropdown at the top right — you are probably in an
archived week. Switch back to the active one.

**A task is not mine but needs changing.** Only the admin or the assignee can change a task. Comment
on it, or ask the admin to reassign it.

**I put a finding on the wrong branch.** Delete it and add it again in the right place; it takes
fifteen seconds.

**The topic turned out to be a dead end.** Say so on Friday, in the meeting prompts. A week that
ends in a well-argued "no" is a good week.
